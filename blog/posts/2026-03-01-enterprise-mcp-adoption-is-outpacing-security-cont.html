The "Wild West" of AI Access

Model Context Protocol (MCP) was designed to make agents talk to data faster. But in the rush for speed, security was left in the dust.

  • Total Permissiveness: MCP servers are often "extremely permissive" and lack basic authentication.
  • The API Nightmare: Experts argue MCP is actually "worse than an API" because it lacks traditional controls.
  • Ghost Identities: We are moving toward hundreds of agents with their own identities and zero human oversight.

Who gets fired when the bot leaks your data?

Traditional security is built for humans. But what happens when a human talks to an AI, that talks to another AI, which then triggers a massive data breach?

The audit trail is a labyrinth. We are operating at a volume and scale that society hasn't even contemplated yet. If an AI mis-authenticates a user, the "gut feeling" of a human security pro is gone. You are left with a logic error that results in a catastrophic leak.

The Illusion of Safety in Legacy Tools

Most enterprises are trying to fight a 21st-century autonomous war with 20th-century muskets. Splunk and other tools offer "fine-grained" access, but they weren't built for the era of agents.

You cannot "guardrail" your way out of a fundamentally broken protocol. If your agents have standing authorization to mutate the state of your production systems, you aren't innovating. You are gambling with the survival of your company.

Right now, you are likely granting "standing authorization" to tools that can auto-discover your most sensitive data. This is a recipe for a headline-making disaster. You need a declarative design where every action is sanctioned and every gate is checked before the aperture is widened.

The speed of AI adoption is outpacing the speed of security controls by a factor of ten. Is your current security framework actually protecting you, or is it just a "Feel Good" measure while the back door is wide open?
